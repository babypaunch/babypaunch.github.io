Whatever you practice, repetition matters.

When practicing a song, listening matters just as much as singing it yourself.

It may be like learning art or photography by looking at good work over and over.

After several listens, I begin to hear a breath, a beat, or the way a note ends that I missed before.

I often suggest that people around me start with songs when they want to learn a foreign language.

The reasons are simple.

1. It is fun.
2. It does not get boring.
3. It is easy to start without pressure.

When you keep listening to a song you like and sing along, unfamiliar sounds slowly become familiar.

You also become more confident about speaking them aloud.

Singing practice works much the same way.

After hearing and singing the same part many times, it eventually comes out naturally without forcing it.

Once it feels familiar, you may even try a little ad-lib.

## I wanted to replay just the part I was learning

Listening to a whole song is lovely, but sometimes a few seconds matter more during practice.

I may have missed the start of a phrase or want to hear how its last note feels again.

Dragging the playback position back every time breaks the flow.

<p class="article-question">Could I easily repeat just the part I want from an MP3 I already have?</p>

Rehearsal MP3 Player is a small player I made to answer that question.

I choose an MP3 on my device and set a Start and End point for each song.

With one-song repeat on, I can keep listening to that section.

If the loop sounds abrupt, Fade-In and Fade-Out make its beginning and end gentler.

**In simple terms: Fade-In and Fade-Out gradually raise and lower the volume.**

I can also switch between the full song and the practice section.

The original file stays untouched, and I can save the practice section as a separate MP3 when I need it.

I also thought about listening with the screen off.

On Android, I can pause or resume from the playback controls on the lock screen.

During practice, I would rather focus on what I hear and sing than on the screen.

## What registering the app made me check

Submitting an app to Google Play involved more than uploading a file.

I had to explain what data the app handles, whether it shows ads, and who it is for.

I made a demo with the real app to show why it keeps playing music when the screen is off.

The app does not provide music or audio files.

People add MP3 files they already have.

Added songs and practice settings stay on the device and are not uploaded to my server.

The Android app may show a banner ad.

It does not request ads for minors or for people who skip the age question.

As of October 6, 2026, I have submitted the app for public Google Play review in South Korea.

Approval and public availability have not yet been confirmed.

## A tool that makes listening again easier

This app is no shortcut to singing well.

It simply lets me return to the part I want to hear until it feels familiar.

Whether I am getting used to the sounds of a foreign-language song or practicing a favorite phrase, I can start lightly.

Enjoying the listen makes me sing along again, and repetition slowly makes the sound my own.

<p class="article-summary"><strong>In one line:</strong> Listening again matters as much as singing again, so I made an MP3 player that makes it easy to return to the section I want to practice.</p>
